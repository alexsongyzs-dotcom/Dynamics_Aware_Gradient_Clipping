\subsection{Completeness, failures, and accuracy}
All 405 planned outcomes pass the source/configuration, split, seed-coverage,
epoch-continuity, calibration-selection, and replay-construction audits.
There are 397 full-budget completions and eight predefined scientific
failures, with no process errors. All eight failures occur without clipping
on FashionMNIST. Its four remaining unclipped runs finish at 10\% accuracy;
retaining failures at zero gives a mean of 3.33\%. Every clipped
confirmation run and every CIFAR-10 run completes its budget.

Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} reports the full comparison. On FashionMNIST, DAGC
reaches 92.41\%, versus 92.60\% for tuned fixed clipping and 92.56\% for the
tuned median rule. On CIFAR-10 at LR 0.1, DAGC reaches 89.50\%, versus
90.67\% for fixed clipping. At LR 0.4, the corresponding scores are 90.87\%
and 91.16\%. AGC attains the highest observed mean at the higher CIFAR
rate, 91.20\%; differences among non-DAGC baselines are not a prespecified
hypothesis family.

The paired DAGC-minus-fixed effects are $-0.18$, $-1.17$, and $-0.29$ points,
with Holm-adjusted $p$-values 0.0176, 0.0044, and 0.0103, respectively
(Table~\ref{tab:paired}). Thus the new study reverses the earlier exploratory
fixed-baseline advantage. These are different data sizes, schedules, and
calibration budgets, so this is a failure of transfer under the new protocol,
not a claim that the earlier measurements were numerically false. The
prespecified broad-superiority criterion is not met.

At CIFAR-10 LR 0.1, calibration selects fixed threshold 5.0, and this threshold
never binds in confirmation. Its norm traces and coefficients exactly match
no clipping. Accordingly, the 1.17-point deficit is also a deficit to the
unmodified optimizer. At LR 0.4, DAGC exceeds no clipping by 0.52 points
[0.25, 0.79], but stronger performance is achieved by the tuned fixed rule.
Avoiding divergence or improving on no clipping is therefore insufficient
evidence for the particular DAGC controller.

\subsection{Replay and alignment controls}
DAGC-minus-replay differences are $+0.02$, $+0.10$, and $-0.07$ points.
Their paired 90\% intervals are [$-0.06$, 0.11], [$-0.04$, 0.25], and
[$-0.18$, 0.04]. Each lies inside the prespecified $\pm0.5$ point margin.
This supports equivalence within each tested condition at that tolerance;
it is not a multiplicity-adjusted familywise equivalence result, nor a claim
that the methods coincide outside these settings. The corresponding 95\%
intervals appear in Table~\ref{tab:paired} and Figure~\ref{fig:paired}.

Removing alignment changes FashionMNIST accuracy by $+0.15$ points in DAGC's
favor, with a paired 95\% interval [$-0.04$, 0.33]. On both CIFAR-10
conditions, all twelve paired raw-gradient norms, applied thresholds,
coefficients, alignment traces, and lower-bound indicators are exactly equal
between DAGC and the no-alignment variant. The complete-trace audit shows that negative smoothed alignments do occur;
the direction term is therefore not intrinsically inactive. All 3,860
negative-alignment-EMA updates across the two CIFAR conditions are projected
to the lower bound. Its effect on
the applied threshold is masked by lower-bound projection in these runs.
This identifies a limit of the implemented controller, rather than
demonstrating that gradient direction never matters.

\subsection{The lower bound dominates the controller}
DAGC's next-step threshold equals its moving lower bound on 97.18\%, 99.07\%,
and 99.00\% of updates, respectively. Its mean clipping intensities are
0.893, 0.900, and 0.899. Figure~\ref{fig:traces} shows the resulting profiles.
These measurements are consistent with the positive-feedback calculation:
clipping stays active and the projection frequently determines the threshold.

The validation-selected median coefficient is 0.1 on FashionMNIST, matching
DAGC's lower-bound scale, and 0.4 on both CIFAR conditions. The CIFAR median
rule therefore retains larger gradients, with intensities near 0.60. DAGC
falls below it by 1.08 points at LR 0.1 (Holm $p=0.0044$) and 0.21 points
at LR 0.4 (Holm $p=0.1914$). The latter comparison does not pass the primary
multiplicity criterion. Bound occupancy describes which part of the rule
is active; by itself it does not prove that changing only the bound would
produce a particular accuracy gain.
