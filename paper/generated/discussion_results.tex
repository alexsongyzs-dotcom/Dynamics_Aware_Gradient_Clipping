The evidence supports a narrow negative conclusion about the frozen V2 rule:
it does not outperform a validation-tuned fixed threshold in any of the three
conditions, and its threshold adaptation adds no practically resolved
advantage over calibration replay under the stated margin. The lower bound
accounts for nearly all threshold updates, while its directional adjustments are masked by
projection on CIFAR-10. Calling these runs successful target tracking or evidence
that online directional feedback is necessary would contradict the controls.
They instead show how an apparently dynamic controller can behave mostly as a
historical-scale clipping rule.

The FashionMNIST and higher-rate CIFAR fixed-baseline gaps are statistically
resolved but smaller than 0.5 points. Statistical inferiority can coexist with
practical equivalence at a specified tolerance; the larger low-rate CIFAR gap
falls outside that tolerance. The finding should guide subsequent research
toward identifying conditions where feedback is informative, rather than
retuning the present confirmation runs after observing their test scores.
