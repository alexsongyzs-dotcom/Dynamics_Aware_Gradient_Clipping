The combined evidence identifies a regime of projection-dominated control.
DAGC spends nearly all threshold updates at its moving lower bound. Negative
alignment EMAs occur on CIFAR-10, but every such update is projected to that
bound, and the paired no-alignment trajectories are identical. This supports
masking by projection in these runs, rather than the stronger claim that
direction is intrinsically uninformative. Calibration replay attains equivalent
accuracy within the prespecified per-condition tolerance; this empirical
result is distinct from the exact same-run replay theorem in the appendix.

The controller loses to tuned fixed clipping in all three conditions. Two
accuracy gaps, on FashionMNIST and higher-rate CIFAR-10, are statistically
resolved yet satisfy the stated $\pm0.5$ point equivalence criterion; the
low-rate CIFAR-10 gap does not. Practical tolerance and statistical difference
answer distinct questions. Neither supplies a benefit-cost argument without
measuring the corresponding costs. Earlier small-subset gains remain valid
observations, but do not establish transfer across the changed data sizes,
schedules, architectures, and baseline selection rules.
